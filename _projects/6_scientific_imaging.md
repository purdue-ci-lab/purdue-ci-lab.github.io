Many scientific questions come down to imaging something that is too fast, too dim, too small, or too faint for a conventional camera. Answering them depends on the whole imaging pipeline: which sensor records the light, how its noise behaves, and how the raw measurements are turned back into the quantity of interest.

We bring the tools developed across the lab, including single-photon and event-based sensors, time-resolved measurements, and physics-based reconstruction, to imaging problems in the sciences. We emphasize accurate forward models of the measurement process, so that reconstructions stay faithful to the physics and their uncertainty is understood.
