Research

We combine computer vision, machine learning, optics, and sensors to recover information hidden from conventional cameras.

High-speed, low-light video Time-of-flight imaging Neural inverse rendering Event-based sensing Foundation & generative models Scientific imaging

Seeing Fast in the Dark

How do we film a scene that is both fast and dark?

At high frame rates or in dim scenes, each frame receives only a handful of photons. We develop sensing models and reconstruction algorithms that recover sharp, high-speed video from these sparse, noisy measurements.

Light in Flight

What does the travel time of light reveal about a scene?

Time-of-flight sensors record not just how much light returns, but when. We use this timing to recover geometry and appearance, from direct depth sensing to hidden objects seen only through scattered light.

Neural Inverse Rendering

Which scene could have produced these measurements?

Inverse rendering runs image formation backward to recover the scene behind a set of measurements. We focus on transient inverse rendering: fitting neural scene representations to time-resolved data, so that the timing of every photon constrains geometry, appearance, and light transport.

Event-Based Sensing

What can a camera see if it only records change?

In a neuromorphic camera, pixels do not record frames; each one responds only to events, changes in the light it measures. Static parts of a scene produce no output, while changes are captured with microsecond timing and high dynamic range. We develop sensing models and algorithms that recover video, motion, and scene structure from these events.

Vision Foundation and Generative Models

How can models trained on ordinary images help us see beyond conventional cameras?

Vision foundation models and generative models learn powerful priors about the visual world from large collections of ordinary images. We adapt these models to unconventional sensors and extreme conditions, such as raw images captured in near darkness, so that their priors carry over to the measurements our cameras actually record.

Scientific Imaging

How can computational cameras help scientists measure what they could not see before?

Many experiments are limited by what their cameras can record. We design sensing and reconstruction methods that capture faster, dimmer, and subtler phenomena, built on accurate physical models of the measurement process.
