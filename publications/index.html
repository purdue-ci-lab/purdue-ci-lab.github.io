  1. ECCV
    Spatiotemporal Flux Probing for Single-Photon Videography
    Jerry Yan*, Matteo Forlivesi*, Bowen Tan, Andrew Xie, Siddharth Somasundaram, and Sotiris Nousias
    In Proceedings of the European Conference on Computer Vision (ECCV), 2026
    • High-speed videography
    • Low-light imaging
    Webpage PDF arXiv Video Supp DOI Abs Bib

    Recovering high-speed video from sparse photon detections requires using structure across both space and time. Spatiotemporal flux probing estimates intensity dynamics directly from photon arrivals, enabling recovery of fast motion and changing illumination with fewer detections. The approach also supports velocity-selective video reconstruction and extends to event and spike camera measurements.

  2. CVPR
    Inter-Photon-Limited Videography
    Andrew Xie, Dongyu Du, Sotiris Nousias, David B. Lindell, and Kiriakos N. Kutulakos
    In Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR), 2026
    • High-speed videography
    • Low-light imaging
    Webpage PDF Abs Bib

    We consider the problem of imaging a dynamic scene when scene appearance variations can outpace photon arrivals. Under such conditions, a pixel is effectively "blind" to changes in appearance that occur within the timespan separating the photons it detects, and so the inter-photon interval presents a significant speed barrier to video acquisition systems. To analyze and advance imaging capabilities at the inter-photon limit, we introduce a novel reparameterization of time-varying flux that reveals the intrinsic difficulty of signal reconstruction by relating the Fourier decomposition of a flux function to the number of photons arriving within each oscillation period. We find that inter-photon-limited videography of general scenes is underexplored and beyond the reach of existing reconstruction techniques. To this end, we introduce Neural Flux Fields—a technique that combines statistical modeling of photon arrival with intrinsic priors of a neural network to achieve robust videography at the inter-photon limit. Using this approach, we demonstrate never-before-seen capabilities in video reconstruction across a range of captured single-photon video datasets spanning the inter-photon-limited regime.

  3. CVPR
    Dark3R: Learning Structure from Motion in the Dark
    Andrew Y. Guo, Anagh Malik, SaiKiran Tedla, Yutong Dai, Yiqian Qin, Zach Salehe, Benjamin Attal, Sotiris Nousias, Kiriakos N. Kutulakos, and David B. Lindell
    In Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR), 2026
    • Low-light imaging
    • 3D reconstruction
    Webpage PDF arXiv Abs Bib

    We introduce Dark3R, a framework for structure from motion in the dark that operates directly on raw images with signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs) below -4 dB–a regime where conventional feature- and learning-based methods break down. Our key insight is to adapt large-scale 3D foundation models to extreme low-light conditions through a teacher-student distillation process, enabling robust feature matching and camera pose estimation in low light. Dark3R requires no 3D supervision; it is trained solely on noisy–clean raw image pairs, which can be either captured directly or synthesized using a simple Poisson-Gaussian noise model applied to well-exposed raw images.To train and evaluate our approach, we introduce a new, exposure-bracketed dataset that includes 42,000 multi-view raw images with ground-truth 3D annotations, and we demonstrate that Dark3R achieves state-of-the-art structure from motion in the low-SNR regime. Further, we demonstrate state-of-the-art novel view synthesis in the dark using Dark3R’s predicted poses and a coarse-to-fine radiance field optimization procedure.

2025

  1. CVPR
    Opportunistic Single-Photon Time of Flight
    Sotiris Nousias*, Mian Wei*, Howard Xiao, Maxx Wu, Shahmeer Athar, Kevin J. Wang, Anagh Malik, David A. Barmherzig, David B. Lindell, and Kiriakos N. Kutulakos
    In Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR), 2025
    • Opportunistic sensing
    • Time-of-flight sensing
    • 3D reconstruction
    Webpage PDF Video Supp DOI Abs Bib

    Pulsed lasers already present in a scene can provide useful illumination for passive three-dimensional imaging. Their characteristic patterns in photon arrival times allow a free-running single-photon camera to detect and separate asynchronous sources, synchronize to them computationally, and recover room-scale depth from the returning light.

2023

  1. ICCV
    Passive Ultra-Wideband Single-Photon Imaging
    Mian Wei*, Sotiris Nousias*, Rahul Gulve, David B. Lindell, and Kiriakos N. Kutulakos
    In Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF International Conference on Computer Vision (ICCV), 2023
    Marr Prize (Best Paper Award)
    • High-speed videography
    • Low-light imaging
    Webpage PDF Code Video Supp DOI Abs Bib

    A free-running single-photon camera can record changing light over timescales from ordinary motion to ultrafast events without synchronizing to a source. This work develops a flux-probing model and a Fourier-domain reconstruction method for sparse photon timestamps, then demonstrates passive video capture across a very wide range of temporal frequencies.

  2. NeurIPS
    Transient Neural Radiance Fields for LiDAR View Synthesis and 3D Reconstruction
    Anagh Malik, Parsa Mirdehghan, Sotiris Nousias, Kiriakos N. Kutulakos, and David B. Lindell
    In Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), 2023
    • Time-of-flight sensing
    • 3D reconstruction
    • Transient rendering
    Webpage PDF arXiv Code Video Supp DOI Abs Bib

    Transient neural radiance fields learn from the time-resolved photon-count histograms produced by single-photon LiDAR. By modeling how light travels through a scene, the method renders transient measurements from new viewpoints and improves geometry and appearance recovery when only a few input views are available.

  1. CVPR
    Large-Scale, Metric Structure from Motion for Unordered Light Fields
    Sotiris Nousias, Manolis Lourakis, and Christos Bergeles
    In Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR), 2019
    • 3D reconstruction
    PDF Video DOI Abs Bib

    This structure-from-motion pipeline reconstructs metric three-dimensional scenes from unordered sets of light-field images. It combines camera-pose recovery with robust triangulation, recovers scene scale, and extends to collections containing hundreds of views.

  2. CVPR
    A Theory of Fermat Paths for Non-Line-of-Sight Shape Reconstruction
    Shumian Xin, Sotiris Nousias, Kiriakos N. Kutulakos, Aswin C. Sankaranarayanan, Srinivasa G. Narasimhan, and Ioannis Gkioulekas
    In Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR), 2019
    Best Paper Award
    • Time-of-flight sensing
    • Non-line-of-sight imaging
    • 3D reconstruction
    Webpage PDF Supp DOI Abs Bib

    Fermat paths of light encode the shape of objects hidden from a transient camera. This work relates discontinuities in transient measurements to the paths’ geometry and uses those constraints to recover hidden surfaces. The resulting method handles objects seen around corners or through diffusers across different transient-imaging systems.

  1. ICCV
    Corner-Based Geometric Calibration of Multi-Focus Plenoptic Cameras
    Sotiris Nousias, François Chadebecq, Jonas Pichat, Pearse Keane, Sébastien Ourselin, and Christos Bergeles
    In Proceedings of the IEEE International Conference on Computer Vision (ICCV), 2017
    PDF Code Video DOI Abs Bib

    This work calibrates multi-focus plenoptic cameras directly from raw images of a checkerboard. It detects corners inside the camera’s micro-images, classifies the micro-lenses, and estimates camera geometry by minimizing reprojection error. The method recovers accurate calibration from a small number of images.
