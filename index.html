Our current work focuses on these areas.

Seeing Fast in the Dark

Capturing video at extreme frame rates and in very low light, where conventional cameras run out of photons.

Explore ↗

Light in Flight

Time-of-flight imaging uses the travel time of light to measure depth, recover 3D shape, and see around corners.

Explore ↗

Neural Inverse Rendering

Neural inverse rendering from time-resolved measurements, recovering geometry, materials, and lighting from how light travels through a scene.

Selected publications

All publications ↗
  1. ECCV
    Spatiotemporal Flux Probing for Single-Photon Videography
    Jerry Yan*, Matteo Forlivesi*, Bowen Tan, Andrew Xie, Siddharth Somasundaram, and Sotiris Nousias
    In Proceedings of the European Conference on Computer Vision (ECCV), 2026
    • High-speed videography
    • Low-light imaging
    Webpage PDF arXiv Video Supp DOI Abs Bib

    Recovering high-speed video from sparse photon detections requires using structure across both space and time. Spatiotemporal flux probing estimates intensity dynamics directly from photon arrivals, enabling recovery of fast motion and changing illumination with fewer detections. The approach also supports velocity-selective video reconstruction and extends to event and spike camera measurements.

  2. ICCV
    Passive Ultra-Wideband Single-Photon Imaging
    Mian Wei*, Sotiris Nousias*, Rahul Gulve, David B. Lindell, and Kiriakos N. Kutulakos
    In Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF International Conference on Computer Vision (ICCV), 2023
    Marr Prize (Best Paper Award)
    • High-speed videography
    • Low-light imaging
    Webpage PDF Code Video Supp DOI Abs Bib

    A free-running single-photon camera can record changing light over timescales from ordinary motion to ultrafast events without synchronizing to a source. This work develops a flux-probing model and a Fourier-domain reconstruction method for sparse photon timestamps, then demonstrates passive video capture across a very wide range of temporal frequencies.

  3. NeurIPS
    Transient Neural Radiance Fields for LiDAR View Synthesis and 3D Reconstruction
    Anagh Malik, Parsa Mirdehghan, Sotiris Nousias, Kiriakos N. Kutulakos, and David B. Lindell
    In Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), 2023
    • Time-of-flight sensing
    • 3D reconstruction
    • Transient rendering
    Webpage PDF arXiv Code Video Supp DOI Abs Bib

    Transient neural radiance fields learn from the time-resolved photon-count histograms produced by single-photon LiDAR. By modeling how light travels through a scene, the method renders transient measurements from new viewpoints and improves geometry and appearance recovery when only a few input views are available.

  4. CVPR
    A Theory of Fermat Paths for Non-Line-of-Sight Shape Reconstruction
    Shumian Xin, Sotiris Nousias, Kiriakos N. Kutulakos, Aswin C. Sankaranarayanan, Srinivasa G. Narasimhan, and Ioannis Gkioulekas
    In Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR), 2019
    Best Paper Award
    • Time-of-flight sensing
    • Non-line-of-sight imaging
    • 3D reconstruction
    Webpage PDF Supp DOI Abs Bib

    Fermat paths of light encode the shape of objects hidden from a transient camera. This work relates discontinuities in transient measurements to the paths’ geometry and uses those constraints to recover hidden surfaces. The resulting method handles objects seen around corners or through diffusers across different transient-imaging systems.
